This is where a lot of first-time investors should start looking. The price of each door is far below what you pay on the peninsula. A duplex here costs about what a house costs, so you can buy two units for the price of one.

That does not make every deal a good deal. The middle half of sales covers a wide range, and the reasons are plain. Unit count matters. Condition matters more. A tired fourplex and a renovated one can trade far apart, and most of that gap is deferred work.

Rents are deep, and the 3-bedroom leases are where most of the action is. If a unit has three bedrooms, you have the biggest pool of comparable rentals to check your rent against. One-bedroom and four-bedroom leases are thinner, so I'd be more careful with those numbers.

Buildings here also sell in about a month, so you don't get a long window to think. Line up your lender and your inspector before you start touring. I'd also run real repair and operating costs, not guesses. Before you buy, I'd look hard at the roof, the wiring, the plumbing, and the flood zone.
