This is the part of North Charleston that sits past the interstate loop, and it reads like a suburb. Think of rows of subdivisions with single-family homes and townhomes, plus some duplexes, built over several decades. Entry prices are lower than inside I-526, and that is the main reason investors look here.

Do not expect many fourplexes. Only a few small buildings sold in the last twelve months, which is too few to plan around. Most of what I see investors buy here is a house or a townhome, and now and then a pair of duplex units. The thing to price is the single rental, one door at a time.

What this area does have is rent data. I have a big pile of lease comps for two-bedroom and three-bedroom homes, so you can check a rent against real signed leases and not a guess. Leases for the smallest and largest homes are fewer, so go easier on those numbers.

The deal here often comes down to the rules, not the building. An HOA can limit rentals, and the address can be in the city or in the county. Sort that out before you fall in love with a house. Looking at a specific house? Send the address my way and we will work through it.
