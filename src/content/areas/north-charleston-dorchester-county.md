Think of this area as the Dorchester County side of the North Charleston job market. A short ride down I-26 puts you at Boeing, the airport, and the rest of the employers around them. That is the reason rental houses here fill, and it is why I look at it as a commuter location first and a neighborhood second.

The buildings are different from what you find inside the old city lines. There are more newer houses and townhomes and fewer old fourplexes. Duplexes do come up, just not often. When one does, it has plenty of company in the single-family listings, so you can compare the building to the house down the street and see what the extra door is worth.

Small multifamily prices here sit close together. The middle half of sales covers a narrow range, which makes a fair price easier to spot. The flip side is speed. Buildings go under contract fast and there is very little on the shelf at any time. If you want one, get your lender ready and set up a search so you see new listings the day they hit.

The two numbers I would check on any deal are the tax bill and the HOA rules. County tax rates are not the same as the city, and a rental can be taxed differently from a home you live in. The HOA can also decide whether you may rent at all. Send me an address and I will run it with you before you spend money on an inspection.
