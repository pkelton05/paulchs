This is where most of the duplex action on the peninsula happens. If you want a small building on the peninsula and you are not buying below the Crosstown, you will probably end up here. The stock is a mix of old cottages that were split into two units, frame houses from the early 1900s, and newer buildings put up as duplexes from day one. Lots are small, and a lot of the older streets now have new infill next to the original homes.

Almost everything that trades is a duplex. A true fourplex is rare. That matters when you read the numbers on this page. The "2–4 unit" median is really a duplex number, so price per door is the cleaner way to compare one building to another. It is also the way to compare a duplex here to a building in North Charleston or West Ashley.

The other thing I tell buyers: duplexes sit longer than houses here. A single-family home tends to move fast. A two-unit building takes much more time to find its buyer, because the price is higher and the pool of people who can underwrite it is smaller. If you do your homework, that gives you room to negotiate and to inspect without a clock running.

Condition drives the deal more than the address does. Two buildings on the same block can be priced far apart, because one has new wiring, plumbing, and a roof and the other is original. Ask for the age of the systems first. Then price the repairs honestly, and run the rent against a real lease comp, not a hope.
