Downtown is the deepest small multifamily market in the region, and also the priciest per door. More buildings change hands here than anywhere else I work. That is the good news. The price of getting in is the other half of the story, and it shapes how I look at every deal below the Crosstown.

The classic building is a Charleston single house that someone split into units years ago. Some are a main house with a rented building behind it. A few are small apartment buildings put up for that purpose. They are old, and age is the whole job. I want to know about the foundation, the termite history, the wiring, the plumbing, and the roof before I think about rent. Exterior work can also need approval from the city, so a cheap fix on paper can turn into a slow one.

Single-family homes here sell for much more than a two- to four-unit building. Large single-family homes set that number, and they are not what you are buying. Compare buildings to buildings, and use price per door when you line one up against another. Rents are high, but I would only trust the one- and two-bedroom numbers on this page. Larger leases mix in furnished and by-the-room deals that do not fit a plain annual lease.

The returns here lean on strong rents and long-term value, not on a big first-year cash check. If that fits your plan, it can work well. If you want cash flow on day one, other parts of the county will serve you better. Send me the building you are looking at and I will run it with you.
